A falaj is the UAE's 3,000-year-old channel system for moving water to where it is needed. This one watches the sea that feeds it.

UAE Swath —
The scan line is where the pushbroom imager is looking right now; the shaded band is everything it has already captured this pass. The instrument sees a 180 km swath at 30 m ground resolution, so one crossing covers the entire UAE coast in a single strip.

Electrical Power —
0.10 m² of triple-junction cells make ~41 W in full sun. The bus draws 6.2 W always; the imager 22 W; the X-band radio 9 W whenever it is keyed — including the polar dump that happens during eclipse, which is the deepest point of the orbit. Over a 33-minute eclipse that is still only a 6 % depth of discharge, which is what a real orbit looks like. The gauge is therefore zoomed to the mission's actual range, and the true percentage is printed beside it.

Water Security — Capacity at Risk JEBEL ALI + FUJAIRAH
The chain, end to end: Chl-a retrieval → intake score → fraction of rated capacity the scheduler will not clear → m³/day → people. This is not a new measurement; it is the same scheduler output in the unit a water authority decides in. "At risk" is capacity an operator would hold back, not an outage — a plant throttling its intake draws reservoir stock first, and population is that volume expressed at the UAE domestic average of 550 L per person per day. The 24-hour peak is the point of the whole mission: without an ocean-colour pass an operator has no forward number at all, and learns about a bloom when pressure across the intake screens rises — by which point it is already inside the plant.

Ground Segment —
Three stations, and none of these timings are scripted: the propagator is swept against each station's 10° horizon mask to find when the spacecraft actually rises. Dubai holds the link through the whole UAE pass; Troll and Inuvik dump the recorder on the polar legs. Countdowns are in real mission time, not demo time — and the transmitter's 9 W shows up in the power budget whenever one of these is green.

Spectroscopy — HYPER-A 96 bands · 400–900 nm
Each bar is one spectral band of reflected light. Algae leave a signature: chlorophyll-a absorbs blue (443 nm) and red (665 nm), reflects green (555 nm), and — the decisive one — re-emits light at 681 nm. That fluorescence peak only appears over living algae, which is why this is worth measuring from orbit rather than inferring from water colour.

Desalination Intake Schedule —
Algal blooms clog reverse-osmosis intakes — the 2008–09 bloom in the Gulf of Oman repeatedly shut Fujairah down. Operators find out when intake pressure drops. This converts the orbital measurement into the only thing they can act on: when to draw water. Hover a bar to see exactly which terms drove that hour's score.

Live Downlink —
Raw telemetry as the ground station receives it, tagged with CCSDS application IDs. Every number here is the same number shown on the gauges — nothing in this log is filler. The line rate rises with activity: one per tick in eclipse, four during a pass.
